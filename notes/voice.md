# Ashley's own words — raw material for the report

Kept verbatim. The Motivation and Impact sections need a real person behind the
problem, and AI-drafted prose reads as AI-drafted prose. These lines go in as
written, or lightly edited by her, never paraphrased into neutral voice.

## Why this project

> "I wanted to build this because I love going out and exploring"

> "but I'm often using google and clicking through multiple aggregators and
> sites to find things to do"

> "this is more of a one stop shop"

> "and can help decide when I'm facing analysis paralysis"

> "or need some inspiration to just get going"

> "it can also help with event and experience discovery"

> "pulling info from places I may not think to"

> "encouraging me to try something new and get out of the rut of visiting the
> same places over and over"

**Three distinct jobs, in her framing — worth structuring the Motivation
section around, because they are not the same problem:**

1. **Aggregation.** The search is spread across Google and a stack of separate
   sites. Cost: time and tab-switching.
2. **Decision.** Even with the options in front of her, choosing is the hard
   part — "analysis paralysis." Cost: the night doesn't happen. This is the one
   the competitors ignore; they all return more options, which makes paralysis
   worse rather than better.
3. **Discovery.** Surfacing things she wouldn't have known to search for —
   "pulling info from places I may not think to."
4. **Breaking the rut.** Actively pushing against returning to the same few
   places. This is a *stated anti-goal for the recommender*: optimising for
   familiarity is what every personalised feed does, and it is the opposite of
   what she wants. Worth building as an explicit novelty bias in the planner and
   naming in the report — it is a design decision competitors have not made.

Job 2 is the strongest novelty claim in the whole project. Every aggregator
optimises for coverage. The Programme commits to one night and defends the
choice, which is a different product, not a better-ranked list.

## The novelty argument — for Report section 3, NOT the website

Cut from the public About page on 2026-10-12 as too explain-y for a visitor.
It is still the sharpest statement of what makes this different, and Section 3
("What is new here", 8 pts) is graded on exactly this:

> A room of eighty and a room of three thousand are not the same night. A yard
> in July and the same venue's back room in January are not the same night. The
> main floor and the room behind it are not the same night.
>
> So we hold the size of every room, whether it sits indoors or out, which of a
> venue's several spaces you would be standing in, and whether they will seat a
> party of twelve.
>
> Genre and neighborhood will only take you so far. The room is what you
> remember.

In the report this needs what the website did not: the named competitors it is
arguing against (Tyra, Sift, Nightlife AI, The Scene, Fever, Clubbable), and
the point that every one of them filters on genre, date, neighborhood and
price, and none on the room.
