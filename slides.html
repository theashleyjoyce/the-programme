The problem

Going out here is a research project

Eater for the restaurant. The Infatuation to check it. Resident Advisor for who is playing. A ticket page. Then a map, to find out whether any of it is near any of the rest.

Nine tabs, four opinions that disagree, and a decision still to make.

So the easy answer wins: the same bar as last time, chosen at eleven o'clock because it was the only one still in your head.

That is not a preference. That is what decision fatigue looks like from the inside.

What is new

Everyone returns options.
We return a night.

A gallery at six. Dinner at half seven. A small room with a DJ at eleven. Somewhere sweet after.

Different kinds of evening, in order, timed so one finishes before the next begins, close enough to walk.

And we model the room. How big it is, indoors or out, which of a venue's several spaces you would be in, whether it seats twelve.

Nowadays is not one venue to us. It is a 269-capacity room and an 800-capacity yard. Different nights.

Compared against Tyra, Sift, Nightlife AI, The Scene, Fever and ChatGPT. None sequence an evening. None know the size of the room.

Safety

32 guardrails, all passing

Three defences that were wrong, and how we found out:

  • The allowlist blocked House of Yes's own booking page. Over-blocking is not safety.
  • A Book button resolved with HTTP 200 and opened the wrong restaurant. Links are now fetched and confirmed.
  • One invented venue used to take down the whole plan with a 500. Now it costs one stop.

What is next

What does not work yet

  • Opening hours. It can place you somewhere closed.
  • No event calendar. It knows venues, not what is on.
  • 507 of 1,319 mapped. The rest show no walking time.
  • Collect hours and refuse to place a closed stop.
  • Add an events feed.
  • Re-check venues on a schedule so closures surface.
  • Put it in front of five people and watch where they get stuck.
